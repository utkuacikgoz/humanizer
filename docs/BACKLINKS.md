# Ownword Backlink Acquisition System (V1)

**Owner:** Backlink Agent
**Status:** V1 operating playbook
**Updated:** 2026-08-23
**Priority:** 2x standard acquisition work; quality and trust gates remain absolute

## 1. Agent charter

The Backlink Agent earns relevant editorial citations by giving writers, educators, editors, researchers, and software publishers something genuinely useful to reference. It does not buy ranking links, automate spam, fake community participation, or trade product access for followed coverage.

### Mission

- Earn reputable, topically relevant referring domains to research, tools, trust resources, and commercial pages.
- Convert outreach intelligence into better assets, product proof, and search pages.
- Build durable relationships and unaided brand mentions, not a pile of low-quality domain metrics.
- Protect the product from link-spam, endorsement, and academic-integrity risk.

### Weekly operating loop

```text
Demand or content gap
-> useful asset/proof
-> qualified prospect list
-> individualized pitch
-> editorial outcome
-> live-link verification
-> traffic/conversion measurement
-> learning fed back into asset
```

The agent owns prospect research, qualification, contact and consent records, outreach, follow-up, placement verification, link-risk review, relationship notes, measurement, and monthly reporting. Product, Humanization, Legal, Copy, and SEO retain approval over product claims, research validity, endorsements, and indexable content.

## 2. Hard rules

Google defines buying links for ranking, product-for-link arrangements, excessive exchanges, automated link creation, and optimized-anchor advertorials as link spam. Any advertising or sponsorship that includes a link must use appropriate `rel="sponsored"` (or at minimum `nofollow` where required) and clear disclosure. Material connections in endorsements must be conspicuous and close to the endorsement under [FTC disclosure guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers). See [Google's spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) for the link-spam baseline.

### Never do

- Pay, gift, discount, donate, or provide product access in exchange for a followed link, ranking anchor, review sentiment, or publication guarantee.
- Purchase guest posts, “authority link” packages, private-blog-network placements, expired domains, link insertions, scholarship links, or bulk directory submissions.
- Use automated contact scraping that violates site terms or applicable privacy/marketing rules.
- Send generic bulk email, rotate domains to evade reputation controls, or continue after opt-out.
- Offer reciprocal links as a condition. Natural cross-references are allowed only when each independently helps readers.
- Seed fake Reddit/forum answers, undisclosed employee posts, fake accounts, fake reviews, or AI-generated personas.
- Target “undetectable,” “bypass Turnitin,” cheating, or guaranteed-detector claims.
- Demand exact-match anchor text or attempt to control an editor's framing.
- Submit rewritten versions of the same guest article to multiple sites.
- Report domain authority/domain rating as if it were a search-engine metric or business outcome.

### Sponsorship and affiliate rule

Paid distribution, creator sponsorship, events, newsletters, and affiliate partnerships can be valid growth channels, but they are not backlink tactics. Contracts must permit independent opinions, require clear disclosure, and require qualifying outbound links where applicable. Track their traffic and revenue separately from earned editorial links.

## 3. Target-page hierarchy

Links should point to the resource that best satisfies the source's readers. Do not force every placement to the homepage.

| Tier | Target | Why someone would cite it | Preferred audience |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Humanization Quality Benchmark | Original reproducible findings, methodology, and data | Researchers, journalists, AI/writing publications, educators |
| 1 | AI Writing Pattern Diagnostic | Functional, useful analysis without authorship accusations | Writing centers, editors, writing newsletters, productivity publications |
| 1 | Meaning-Preservation Checklist | Practical reference protecting facts and citations | Editors, researchers, librarians, writing programs |
| 1 | AI Writing Patterns Field Guide | Annotated examples and clear definitions | Teachers, content teams, ESL resources, creators |
| 2 | Semantic Verification / protected-content explanation | Transparent product method and limitations | Technical reviewers, AI tooling roundups, professional writers |
| 2 | Maintained category comparison | Firsthand, dated test data | Software reviewers, buyer guides, newsletters |
| 3 | Relevant mode page | Direct product fit after genuine review or mention | Professional/academic/creator niche resources |
| 3 | Homepage | Entity citation or broad product recommendation | Directories, profiles, general coverage |

Target distribution for the first 90 days: at least 70% of active outreach to Tier 1 assets, no more than 20% to Tier 2, and no more than 10% to direct commercial/homepage pitches. Earned editors decide the final URL and anchor.

## 4. Linkable-asset roadmap

### Asset 1: AI Writing Pattern Diagnostic

**Hook:** A privacy-conscious diagnostic that identifies repetitive openings, sentence-length uniformity, generic transitions, corporate filler, parallel structures, and likely protected entities. It explicitly does not claim to determine whether a human or AI wrote the text.

**Linkable components:** live tool, methodology, sample output, classroom/editor worksheet, accessible static explanation, citation snippet, privacy note.

**Outreach triggers:** a publisher has an outdated “signs of AI writing” article; a writing center teaches revision; an editor shares before/after examples; a newsletter covers responsible AI workflows.

**Release gate:** the tool works, no text is stored by default, claims match actual analysis, mobile/accessibility QA passes, and share links never expose source text.

### Asset 2: Humanization Quality Benchmark

**Hook:** A transparent benchmark that measures not just naturalness but semantic failures, protected-content failures, latency, token cost, and retries across 100 categorized passages.

**Linkable components:** methods page, results, failure taxonomy, data dictionary, downloadable aggregate data, charts, changelog, reproducibility notes, expert commentary.

**Outreach triggers:** new benchmark version; surprising category-specific failure; model update; comparison methodology discussion; journalist request for detector-alternative framing.

**Release gate:** methods are frozen before results, sample provenance is documented, customer text is excluded, limitations are prominent, claims survive Humanization/Legal review, and chart data is reproducible.

### Asset 3: Meaning-Preservation Checklist

**Hook:** A concise editorial checklist for verifying entities, numbers, dates, quotations, citations, terminology, relationships, claims, and conclusions after any rewrite.

**Linkable components:** canonical web version, accessible one-page PDF, printer-friendly version, permissive classroom/team-use terms, version/date.

**Outreach triggers:** writing center resource lists, editorial QA guides, research communication curricula, professional writing templates.

### Asset 4: AI Writing Patterns Field Guide

**Hook:** An evidence-led guide to robotic patterns with annotated counterexamples, explaining that patterns are editing signals rather than proof of authorship.

**Linkable components:** stable definitions, original before/after examples, downloadable visual cards, expert quotes, pattern glossary.

### Asset 5: State of AI-Assisted Writing report

Do not produce this in the first 60 days. Launch only when the dataset is large and representative enough for honest analysis, privacy review supports aggregation, and a statistician or methodologist has reviewed claims. A recurring report is a citation event, not a pretext for invented trend data.

### Category sequencing

1. Start with relationship-friendly, high-relevance prospects: writing centers, editors, newsletters, responsible-AI groups.
2. Use genuine usage feedback to improve assets.
3. Approach journalists/researchers only when the benchmark provides a defensible news hook.
4. Approach reviewers and directories after final brand/domain, pricing, privacy, and product access are stable.
5. Scale no category until reply quality and placement relevance demonstrate fit.

## 6. Prospect discovery

Each prospect must be tied to a specific page, person, and reason. Discovery methods include:

- Search for curated resource pages, syllabi, toolkits, link roundups, and editorial guides relevant to writing revision, AI literacy, semantic preservation, and content quality.
- Review authors and publishers already citing related primary research or maintaining category comparisons.
- Monitor unlinked brand mentions only after the final name is live.
- Find broken links only where the new asset genuinely replaces the missing reader value.
- Review competitor referring pages for category discovery, never for blind cloning or mass contact.
- Track conference agendas, editorial calendars, model releases, academic terms, and benchmark updates for timely angles.
- Convert inbound support/interview questions into anonymized resource ideas with permission.

Do not harvest personal emails from private sources. Prefer published professional contacts, publication forms, or verified business addresses. Keep a source URL and lawful-basis/consent note where required.

## 7. Qualification rubric

Score every prospect before contact. A prospect needs **>= 70/100** and no automatic disqualifier. Scores guide prioritization; they do not justify spam.

| Dimension | Weight | Full-score test |
|---|---:|---|
| Topical relevance | 25 | The exact audience actively uses or cites writing/AI revision resources |
| Editorial legitimacy | 15 | Real owner/authors, original editorial content, clear contact/corrections process |
| Asset-to-page fit | 15 | One specific live page would be materially improved by the proposed resource |
| Audience quality | 10 | Human audience aligns with paid user or credible citation ecosystem |
| Evidence of real readership | 10 | Recent updates, discussion, subscribers/traffic evidence, citations, or community activity |
| Outbound-link quality | 10 | Links are contextual and useful; no obvious casino/loan/link-market pattern |
| Brand/trust alignment | 10 | Responsible AI, writing quality, privacy, and integrity framing are compatible |
| Contact confidence | 5 | Correct editor/owner and legitimate professional route are identified |

### Automatic disqualifiers

- Publishes prices for followed links, paid guest posts, link insertions, or guaranteed rankings.
- Obvious PBN, scraped/AI-spun site, expired-domain repurpose, or unrelated topic sprawl.
- Thin directory with no curation or real audience.
- Requires exact-match anchors, reciprocal link, positive review, or editorial control.
- Promotes cheating, plagiarism, malware, piracy, adult/gambling/loan spam, or deceptive detector bypass.
- Hides ownership, authorship, or material connections.
- Contact obtained through a prohibited or private source.

### Quality bands

- **85-100 / A:** Manual research and founder/expert outreach; maximum 10 new contacts/week.
- **70-84 / B:** Individualized agent outreach; maximum 25 new contacts/week.
- **55-69 / nurture:** Do not pitch. Follow, learn, and reassess after asset or relationship improves.
- **<55 / reject:** Do not contact.

No metric from a third-party SEO vendor can override an automatic disqualifier.

## 8. Prospect record and pipeline

Required fields:

- Prospect organization, publication, URL, country, category, and audience
- Specific target page and why the asset improves it
- Contact name/role, published contact source, address type, and consent/legal note
- Rubric score with evidence for each dimension
- Proposed asset, pitch angle, proof point, and personalization line
- Relationship owner and prior interactions
- First contact date, follow-up dates, status, response, objection, and opt-out
- Placement URL, target URL, anchor, `rel` attribute, disclosure, first-live date, last-checked date
- Referral sessions, qualified starts, paid/assisted conversions, citations, and notes
- Risk review and remediation status

Pipeline statuses: `researched -> qualified -> approved -> contacted -> replied -> collaborating -> earned -> declined -> nurture -> do-not-contact -> lost/removed`.

Deduplicate by organization, person, domain, and target page before every send. A global do-not-contact flag overrides every campaign.

## 9. Outreach principles

- Personalize around the recipient's audience and a specific page, not superficial compliments.
- Lead with the useful fact, tool, replacement, or finding. Product mention comes second.
- Keep first contact below roughly 120 words unless responding to a journalist request.
- Use one clear, low-friction ask. Never ask for “a dofollow backlink.”
- State affiliations and material connections plainly.
- Do not attach files on cold outreach; link to a safe public resource.
- Maximum two follow-ups. Stop immediately on opt-out, negative reply, or role mismatch.
- Suggested cadence: initial, follow-up after 4-5 business days, final follow-up after 7-10 more business days.
- Do not manufacture urgency. News hooks must be real and dated.
- If the asset does not clearly improve the recipient's content, do not send.

## 10. Outreach sequences and templates

Templates are scaffolds. Bracketed fields require real research; sending a template with generic or invented personalization fails the quality gate.

### Sequence A: resource-page suggestion

**Subject:** Resource for `[specific page/topic]`

Hi `[name]` — your `[page title]` helps `[audience]` with `[specific task]`. One gap I noticed is a practical way to verify that a rewrite kept its facts, numbers, citations, and conclusions intact.

We published a free `[meaning-preservation checklist/diagnostic]` for exactly that review step: `[URL]`. It includes `[one concrete differentiator]` and does not make AI-authorship or detector-bypass claims.

If it would genuinely help your readers, would you consider reviewing it for the page? Either way, I’d value corrections from your team.

`[real name, role, product affiliation]`

**Follow-up 1:**

Hi `[name]` — one concrete section that may fit `[page]` is `[section/feature]`, because `[audience-specific reason]`. No need to reply if it is not a match; I wanted to make the review easy. `[URL]`

**Final follow-up:**

Closing the loop on this. If `[asset]` is not useful for `[page/audience]`, I will not follow up again. If a different format would be more helpful, I’m glad to note it for our next update.

### Sequence B: benchmark/data pitch

**Subject:** Data: where AI rewrites change meaning

Hi `[name]` — for your coverage of `[specific beat/article]`, we tested `[scope]` across `[categories]` and tracked semantic failures, protected-content failures, naturalness, latency, and cost.

The most relevant finding for your audience is `[precise, defensible finding with sample/base]`. Methods, limitations, and downloadable aggregate data are here: `[URL]`.

I can provide the underlying chart, failure examples we have rights to share, or a methods interview with `[qualified person]`. I work on `[product]`; no coverage or link is expected.

`[signature]`

**Follow-up:** only when there is a genuinely new chart, correction, version, or timely angle. Never resend the same pitch as “bumping this.”

### Sequence C: broken/outdated resource

**Subject:** Outdated resource on `[page title]`

Hi `[name]` — while using `[page]`, I found that `[linked resource/tool]` now `[returns 404 / makes an outdated claim]` at `[location on page]`.

If you’re updating it, this `[tool/guide]` covers `[same reader job]` and adds `[specific value]`: `[URL]`. We maintain it and show its method, privacy behavior, and update date.

The broken-link note stands regardless of whether our resource fits. Thanks for maintaining the page.

`[signature]`

### Sequence D: expert contribution, not guest-post barter

**Subject:** Source for `[planned/recent topic]`

Hi `[name]` — I saw your work on `[specific topic]`. Our benchmark focuses on a failure that gets missed in “humanizer” discussions: rewrites can sound smoother while changing facts, dates, citations, or conclusions.

If useful for future coverage, `[qualified expert]` can share `[specific firsthand evidence]`, including failures and limitations—not a prewritten promotional article. Methods: `[URL]`.

For transparency, we build a paid writing product. You retain full editorial control, and no link or favorable mention is expected.

### Sequence E: unlinked brand mention

Use only after the final name and canonical domain are stable.

**Subject:** Source link for `[article title]`

Hi `[name]` — thank you for mentioning `[product]` in `[article]`. The canonical source for `[fact/tool/result discussed]` is `[URL]`, in case adding it would help readers verify the detail.

One factual update: `[only if needed]`. No change is needed if a link would not improve the piece.

`[signature]`

### Sequence F: reviewer access

**Subject:** Independent access for `[publication]` testing

Hi `[name]` — your `[comparison/review]` evaluates `[relevant criterion]`. If you plan to update it, we can provide temporary product access for independent testing, with no requirement for coverage, sentiment, or a link.

The areas that are most testable are protected content, meaning verification, sentence regeneration, and the four writing modes. Product facts and limitations: `[URL]`.

Any complimentary access must be disclosed under your policy and applicable law. We do not request followed links from compensated or gifted coverage.

`[signature]`

## 11. Relationship and contribution plays

### Writing-center advisory circle

Invite 5-8 writing tutors, editors, librarians, or educators to criticize the checklist and diagnostic. Participation must not require promotion. Compensate advisors for labor if appropriate, disclose it on the resource, and treat any resulting coverage as independent with appropriate link qualification. Publish substantive corrections and credit only with permission.

### Editor workshop

Offer a live, non-promotional workshop on verifying facts and citations after AI-assisted revision. Give attendees the checklist. Do not condition the workshop, recording, or access on a backlink. Links from host resource pages are an editorial choice.

### Research commentary desk

Maintain a response SLA and evidence pack for journalists: expert bios, methods, charts, licensing terms, limitations, correction policy, and product disclosure. Answer only within actual expertise.

### Co-created assets

Co-create only when both parties contribute material expertise and the result has independent reader value. Put authorship, funding, methodology, and relationships on the asset. Never disguise a paid placement as independent research.

## 12. Measurement and KPIs

### Primary KPIs

- New qualified editorial referring domains, excluding paid/sponsored/UGC links.
- Live-link retention at 30/90/180 days.
- Referral visitors who start a humanization, view a preview, become paid, and return for second use.
- Organic lift and assisted conversions to the linked target page, interpreted cautiously rather than claimed as link causality.
- Verifiable citations/mentions in research, editorial, educational, and AI-search contexts.
- Relationship yield: repeat replies, invitations, expert requests, and co-creation opportunities.

### Efficiency KPIs

- Qualified prospects researched per hour.
- Positive response rate = relevant positive replies / delivered individualized pitches.
- Placement rate = earned editorial placements / delivered individualized pitches.
- Time from qualified to live placement.
- Cost per qualified referring domain, including labor and asset costs but excluding paid distribution from earned-link reporting.
- Asset-level yield: qualified domains, referral starts, conversions, and citations per asset.

### Risk KPIs

- Spam complaints, opt-out violations, bounced messages, and duplicate-contact incidents.
- Percentage of new links from disqualified or irrelevant sites.
- Undisclosed paid/gifted placements.
- Manipulative anchor concentration.
- Lost links caused by obsolete URLs or neglected assets.
- Manual actions or credible publisher complaints.

### Initial 90-day targets

These are operating targets, not forecasts; recalibrate after the first 50 delivered pitches.

- Build 150 researched records; qualify 60 at >=70; send no more than 75 individualized first contacts.
- Achieve >=15% relevant positive response rate and >=8% earned-placement rate.
- Earn 8-12 qualified editorial referring domains across at least four prospect categories.
- At least 70% of earned links point to Tier 1 assets and at least 80% use natural editorial anchors chosen by the publisher.
- Maintain 100% disclosure/qualification compliance, 0 paid-followed links, 0 fake reviews, 0 opt-out violations, and <3% hard-bounce rate.
- Verify 100% of live links and recheck at day 30 and day 90.
- Attribute referral product starts and paid/assisted outcomes; do not set a conversion target until traffic reaches a useful sample.

## 13. Link verification and risk response

### On placement

- Verify the exact referring URL, HTTP status, rendered link, target, anchor, `rel`, disclosure, indexability, and page relevance.
- Screenshot or archive the placement for internal evidence where permitted.
- Thank the editor without asking for anchor or `rel` changes unless a factual URL is broken.
- Record the relationship and next useful follow-up, not an immediate second ask.

### Monthly link audit

- Separate earned editorial, organic directory/profile, UGC, sponsored/affiliate, and unknown links.
- Flag irrelevant networks, sitewide template links, exact-match clusters, sudden bursts, hacked pages, and links from automatic disqualifiers.
- Investigate whether internal staff, contractors, affiliates, agencies, or partners caused questionable links.
- End and document the source before considering technical remediation.
- Request removal/qualification for links the company created or paid for.
- Do not routinely disavow random spam. Consider Google's disavow process only for a manual action or a substantial body of manipulative links that cannot be removed, with SEO and Legal approval and a documented file review.

### Incident severity

| Severity | Example | Action |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Paid followed placement, fake review, undisclosed endorsement, PBN campaign, manual action | Stop campaign immediately; notify SEO/Product/Legal; preserve records; request removal/qualification; assess Search Console and disclosure remediation |
| S2 | Contractor sends spam, ignores opt-out, or targets disqualified sites | Pause sender/account; suppress affected contacts; audit batch; apologize where appropriate; retrain or terminate vendor |
| S3 | Natural spam link, scraper, irrelevant unaffiliated mention | Record and monitor; no outreach or disavow by default |
| S4 | Link lost through normal editorial update | Check target/value; offer updated source only if useful; otherwise accept editorial decision |

## 14. 30/60/90-day execution plan

### Days 0-30: prepare assets and a clean prospect base

- Confirm final canonical domain before active directory/reviewer outreach; prospect research can proceed under the codename.
- Define the backlink CRM schema, suppression list, rubric, reporting view, and UTM convention.
- Create 75 prospect records across writing centers, editors, responsible-AI groups, newsletters, researchers, and specialist press; qualify the first 30.
- Review every public claim and link target; do not pitch placeholders, gated customer results, or unfinished tools.
- Prepare the diagnostic, checklist, field guide, and benchmark media/source packs.
- Conduct 5-8 no-promotion feedback interviews to improve the first assets.
- Send a maximum of 15 high-confidence relationship-first emails after an asset passes release gates.

### Days 31-60: run focused campaigns

- Launch resource-page outreach for the diagnostic/checklist and educator/editor outreach for the field guide.
- Send benchmark pitches only after methods and results pass review.
- Test two angles per asset with small, manually reviewed cohorts; change one variable at a time.
- Verify every placement, record objections, and improve the asset when feedback repeats.
- Add ethical directories and independent reviewers only after product identity, pricing, and privacy facts are stable.
- Publish the first monthly report: qualified domains, replies, placements, referral starts, risk events, and asset decisions.

### Days 61-90: deepen relationships and compound winners

- Expand only the top two prospect/asset combinations by positive-reply quality and qualified placements.
- Offer evidence-led workshops, methods interviews, and source access to existing relationships.
- Run unlinked-mention reclamation if the final brand has enough mentions.
- Refresh failed pitches by improving the resource; do not compensate with higher volume.
- Recheck all earned links at 30/90 days and recover broken targets with redirects or useful updates.
- Produce the next-quarter roadmap based on retained paid users and qualified citations, not link count alone.

## 16. Pre-send and post-placement checklists

### Pre-send

- [ ] Recipient, role, page, and contact source are correct.
- [ ] Prospect score is >=70 with no disqualifier.
- [ ] The asset is live, useful, accurate, and relevant to the cited page.
- [ ] The message includes one real audience-specific reason and no invented praise.
- [ ] Every claim is traceable to approved evidence.
- [ ] Sender identity and product affiliation are clear.
- [ ] No payment, gift, positive sentiment, followed link, or anchor is requested.
- [ ] Suppression, deduplication, and regional outreach rules pass.
- [ ] One ask, working canonical URL, and appropriate tracking are present.
- [ ] A human approved the final message.

### Post-placement

- [ ] Placement is editorially relevant and factually accurate.
- [ ] URL, status, anchor, target, `rel`, disclosure, and live date are recorded.
- [ ] Paid/gifted relationship, if any, is conspicuously disclosed and link-qualified.
- [ ] No confidential/customer text or unsupported claim appears.
- [ ] Thank-you contains no new link demand.
- [ ] Referral and conversion tracking works without sensitive data.
- [ ] 30-day and 90-day verification is scheduled.

## 17. Key risks and mitigations

| Risk | Early signal | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Category normalizes paid links | Editors quote prices or guarantee placement | Reject, record disqualifier, and do not negotiate for `nofollow` as a disguised SEO buy |
| Outreach becomes volume-driven | Generic messages, falling reply quality, complaints | Enforce cohort caps, human approval, >=70 threshold, and asset improvement before scale |
| Academic links frame the product as cheating | Evasion anchors, detector claims, student-policy complaints | Require integrity framing, refuse/seek correction of deceptive claims, stop that prospect segment if repeated |
| Weak benchmark undermines credibility | Method questions cannot be answered; data changes silently | Freeze methods, version releases, publish limitations/changelog, use qualified reviewer |
| Review access becomes an endorsement | Positive coverage or followed link expected for free access | Written no-obligation terms, disclosure, independent testing, qualified links where required |
| Final brand/domain changes after links | Lost equity and broken references | Delay broad outreach; centralize identity; preserve old domain and one-hop redirects if migration occurs |
| Contractor creates hidden spam | Sudden exact-match links, unrelated domains, opaque invoices | Contractual prohibitions, raw placement access, monthly audit, immediate pause/remediation |
| Random scraper/spam links cause panic | Low-quality domains appear without outreach | Monitor; do not pay, contact, or routinely disavow; escalate only with substantial manipulative pattern/manual action |
| “GEO mentions” vendors sell inauthentic placements | Promised AI citations or undisclosed forum mentions | Reject; Google explicitly warns against inauthentic mentions; invest in source-worthy evidence |

## 18. Definition of done for V1 backlink system

V1 is operational when:

- The CRM, qualification, suppression, disclosure, incident, and reporting systems are live.
- At least two Tier 1 assets pass product, privacy, claims, accessibility, and editorial release gates.
- The first 60 qualified prospects have complete, evidence-backed records.
- Individualized pilot outreach has been completed without spam, disclosure, or opt-out violations.
- Earned placements are verified and connected to the privacy-safe product funnel.
- Monthly reporting can distinguish editorial authority and retained paid users from vanity link counts.
- Every participant can state the hard rule: no money, product, gift, review access, or reciprocity in exchange for a followed link.
